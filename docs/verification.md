## Pilot findings (exploratory, not significance claims)

On the fixed OOD trace panel, calibrated gating reduced mean violation fraction from **0.1931** (identical ungated RL) to **0.1193**, with mean QoE increasing from **−0.0433** to **0.5997**. This is not a universal win: GCC-like achieved **0.0674** violations and **1.0469** QoE, while raw-confidence gating achieved **0.1142** violations. All are retained in the generated table.

On the main method's identical proposal/state rows, pooled ID NLL changed from **0.02741 raw** to **0.02560 reported**; pooled OOD NLL worsened from **0.32784 raw** to **0.41461 reported**. This illustrates why ID calibration is not treated as an OOD safety guarantee. These are descriptive pilot estimates; use the raw CSV and paired intervals rather than rounded prose for analysis.

The final pilot exercised low-confidence, OOD-support, missing-feedback and hysteresis fallback reasons. It also includes physically unavoidable failures; the fallback is conservative, not infallible. Very large reported outage latency values are the documented virtual-delay penalty, not measured packet latency.
