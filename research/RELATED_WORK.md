# Research consulted and design implications

This is an engineering framework grounded in the sources below, **not a claim of a new proven-safe RL algorithm or a reproduction of their results**. The actual implementation and synthetic assumptions are specified in `docs/methodology.md`.

## Source-to-design map

1. **Guo, Pleiss, Sun & Weinberger (ICML 2017), On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks**, [arXiv 1706.04599](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599), [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html). Read §2 calibration/ECE, §4.1 binary Platt scaling and §4.2 temperature scaling. Post-hoc calibration uses a held-out set and assumes compatible train/test distributions. Here we calibrate a binary action-safety predictor, not a softmax over Q-values. We evaluate NLL/Brier and bin counts alongside ECE, and retain a temperature ablation.
2. **van Hasselt, Guez & Silver (AAAI 2016), Deep Reinforcement Learning with Double Q-learning**, [arXiv 1509.06461](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.06461). Separating online action selection from target-network action evaluation motivates our Double DQN target. This is a small NumPy implementation, not their Atari architecture/benchmark.
3. **Jay, Rotman, Godfrey, Schapira & Tamar (ICML 2019), A Deep Reinforcement Learning Perspective on Internet Congestion Control**, [PMLR 97:3050–3059](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/jay19a.html). The abstract motivates congestion-control RL and explicitly identifies fairness, safety and generalization as adoption challenges. We provide a seeded testbed but do not implement Aurora or claim its published gains.
4. **Lakshminarayanan, Pritzel & Blundell (NeurIPS 2017), Simple and Scalable Predictive Uncertainty Estimation using Deep Ensembles**, [arXiv 1612.01474](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01474). HF metadata/abstract motivated multiple independently initialized predictors. Our episode-bootstrap ensemble is an adaptation for correlated safety data, not a Bayesian posterior or calibrated interval. Ensemble disagreement is logged separately.
5. **Ovadia et al. (NeurIPS 2019), Can You Trust Your Model's Uncertainty? Evaluating Predictive Uncertainty Under Dataset Shift**, [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/1906.02530). The abstract/introduction explicitly report shortcomings of post-hoc calibration under shift. We separate ID/OOD evaluation, preserve raw versus calibrated scores on the same states, and never infer a safety guarantee from an ID reliability diagram.
6. **Holmer et al. (2016), A Google Congestion Control Algorithm for Real-Time Communication**, [draft-ietf-rmcat-gcc-02](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-ietf-rmcat-gcc-02). The draft combines delay-based overuse detection and rate control with loss-based control. Read the rate-increase/decrease discussion. Our simplified AIMD baseline deliberately omits the full delay estimator/packet machinery and has different probe constants. The draft is historical work in progress, not an RFC standard or a specification of today's production libwebrtc.
7. **Jesup & Sarker (2021), RFC 8836: Congestion Control Requirements for Interactive Real-Time Media**, [RFC Editor](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8836). Sections 1–2 motivate low delay, rapid adaptation, stability, feedback handling and fairness. The RFC does not specify our 150 ms/5% safety thresholds. Those are declared experimental design choices. Our single-flow simulator cannot assess its fairness requirements.

## Additional sources for the RLCD manuscript

8. **Geifman & El-Yaniv (ICML 2019), SelectiveNet: A Deep Neural Network with an Integrated Reject Option**, [PMLR 97:2151–2159](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/geifman19a.html). HF search identified arXiv 1901.09192; `hf papers read` returned not found, so the official PMLR abstract and citation were read. SelectiveNet jointly trains prediction and rejection. RLCD instead post-hoc calibrates action-safety predictions and uses a stateful fallback; its probability-only risk/coverage plots are descriptive and not claims to implement SelectiveNet.
9. **Alshiekh et al. (2017 arXiv version), Safe Reinforcement Learning via Shielding**, [arXiv 1708.08611v2](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.08611v2). HF info/read did not resolve this paper, so its arXiv abstract, introduction and §4 safety-specification/abstraction discussion were read directly. Shield synthesis uses a temporal-logic safety specification and a conservative abstraction of environment dynamics to enforce safe behavior. RLCD has neither that abstraction nor a winning-region invariant. Its calibrated one-step gate must not be called a formal shield or inherit shielding guarantees. The preprint version is cited explicitly rather than inventing conference metadata.

A broad HF search for Neural Simplex returned unrelated architecture papers, which were not used as sources. A broad shielding search returned other, later papers, which were not substituted for the primary work. The V7 uncertainty-screen literature search used `hf papers search "reinforcement learning safety shield epistemic uncertainty ensemble disagreement action selection" --limit 5`, followed by full-text `hf papers read 1907.01475 --format human`.

### Ensemble disagreement as an action-abstention signal (V7 motivation)

17. **Kenton, Filos, Evans & Gal (2019), Generalizing from a few environments in safety-critical reinforcement learning**, [arXiv 1907.01475](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.01475). Retrieved and read in full with `hf papers read 1907.01475 --format human` after an HF Papers search for ensemble disagreement and safe RL. The results are deliberately mixed: simple ensemble averaging and a catastrophe blocker reduce catastrophes in their gridworld but do not significantly reduce them in the harder CoinRun setting; ensemble-value disagreement still helps rank next-step catastrophe risk for possible intervention in some settings. This motivates an empirical disagreement-based abstention experiment only. It does not validate our action screen, establish OOD detection, or provide a safety guarantee. V7 compares the existing calibrated top-five media screen with the same screen plus a fixed standard-deviation cutoff using new validation and test traces.

### Delay evidence versus raw-loss backoff (V9)

18. **Singh, Ott & Holmer (2021), RFC 8868: Evaluating Congestion Control for Interactive Real-Time Media**, [RFC Editor](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8868.html). Consulted the introduction's real-codec/quality caveat and sections 4.2--4.4 on end-to-end loss, queueing and independent/correlated loss models. Citation initials, date and DOI were checked against [RFC Editor BibXML](https://www.rfc-editor.org/refs/bibxml/reference.RFC.8868.xml). These motivate evaluating loss and delay separately, not assuming raw loss alone identifies congestion or that a delay-only heuristic classifies loss causes reliably. V9 changes both warm-budget and fallback loss response, retains FEC preference and learned Q/support, and freezes new validation/test IDs. It claims neither RFC test-suite compliance nor real-codec or safety validation.

Retrieval: `hf papers search "real-time congestion control delay loss" --limit 3 --format json` returned unrelated model-serving material, which was not cited. `hf papers info 2408.03007 --format json` returned not found; no full-text or results claim is made for that paper. Official RFC passages/BibXML were retrieved directly and cited rather than substituting unrelated Hub results.

## Testable questions, not assumed conclusions

- Does gating reduce violations relative to the identical ungated policy, and at what QoE/fallback cost?
- Does post-hoc scaling improve held-out ID NLL/Brier/ECE on identical proposal-state pairs? Does that improvement persist under OOD shift?
- What changes when support rejection, hysteresis, ensemble diversity or FEC is removed?
- Is GCC-like control still stronger than learned control for some/all metrics? Always report it.
- When are violations physically unavoidable, and when does the fallback itself introduce harm?

No novelty, superiority, statistical significance or real-world safety claim follows automatically from this framework.

### Conformal risk control and the v3 threshold study

11. **Angelopoulos et al. (2022 arXiv version), Conformal Risk Control**, [arXiv 2208.02814](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.02814). Retrieved using `hf papers read 2208.02814 --format human`; read the abstract, Theorem 2.1 setup and discussion of non-monotone risk. CRC controls expected bounded losses for exchangeable random loss functions that are non-increasing in the selected parameter; the paper explicitly notes the guarantee fails for non-monotone risk. A hysteretic gate changes state/action trajectories as its threshold varies, so per-episode violations need not be monotone. Time steps are serially dependent. We therefore use validation-only empirical model selection in v3 and make no CRC guarantee.
12. **Farinhas, Zerva, Ulmer & Martins (2023 arXiv version), Non-Exchangeable Conformal Risk Control**, [arXiv 2310.01262](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01262). Retrieved using `hf papers read 2310.01262 --format human`. This work broadens CRC to non-exchangeable sampling with relevance weighting but retains monotone bounded-loss structure and requires a justified calibration/target sampling relationship. We have not established such assumptions or weights for this heterogeneous closed-loop simulator and do not call v3 non-X CRC. The paper clarifies assumptions not met; it gives no guarantee to this controller.



10. **Tobin et al. (2017 arXiv version), Domain Randomization for Transferring Deep Neural Networks from Simulation to the Real World**, [arXiv 1703.06907](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06907). Located with `hf papers search "Domain Randomization for Transferring Deep Neural Networks from Simulation to the Real World" --limit 2 --format json`; retrieved with `hf papers read 1703.06907 --format human`. Read the abstract/introduction, §III domain-randomization procedure and conclusion. The source randomizes visual scene/camera/rendering properties and validates robot object localization; it does not validate network adaptation. It motivates broadening synthetic safety-training coverage, not any transfer or novelty claim. Our v2 data randomizes capacity, base RTT, queue scale, loss and feedback timing. Because those mechanisms now overlap the old OOD panel, we call that panel **stress**, not novel-mechanism OOD for the refitted model.

The exact refit/selection design was frozen in `docs/IMPROVEMENT_V2.md` and `configs/improve_v2.json` before candidate evaluation. Policy weights are unchanged; ID-only refitting controls for richer action supervision, and shared stress-predictor weights isolate fallback composition. New final traces are used only after validation selection is persisted.

### Fixed policy-training randomization (v4)

13. **Tiboni, Klink, Peters, Tommasi, D'Eramo & Chalvatzaki (2023 arXiv version), Domain Randomization via Entropy Maximization**, [arXiv 2311.01885](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01885). Retrieved with `hf papers read 2311.01885 --format human`; read the problem formulation, entropy-maximization objective, adaptive domain-randomization method and reported sim-to-real robotics experiments. It motivates treating the training distribution as an experimental variable and cautions against assuming that indiscriminately broad randomization is optimal. Its method/domains are robotic, not media networks, and do not establish any result for our simulator. V4 therefore tests one fixed, preregistered 35% stress-scenario policy-training mixture at the original training budget; it does not implement their adaptive method. Safety-model risk fitting and probability calibration remain ID-only, and all stress scenario families are exposed during policy training, so v4 stress evaluation is **not** a held-out-mechanism OOD claim. `docs/POLICY_RANDOMIZATION_V4.md` freezes the schedule, promotion constraints and validation-before-test ordering.

### Action-level chance screens (v5)

14. **Chen et al. (2023 arXiv version), Safe Reinforcement Learning via Hierarchical Adaptive Chance-Constraint Safeguards**, [arXiv 2310.03379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03379). Retrieved from Hugging Face with `hf papers search "risk-sensitive reinforcement learning CVaR constrained policy optimization" --limit 5 --format json` and `hf papers read 2310.03379`; the full arXiv HTML was read. ACS evaluates actions against a chance constraint and projects into a feasible set, but its method also uses a learned recovery policy and derives guarantees under its formal assumptions. This motivates a *much narrower empirical ablation*: rank the five highest-Q media actions, accept the first whose existing calibrated one-step safety score passes the frozen 0.90 threshold and whose telemetry support is accepted, otherwise use the existing deterministic fallback. We do not implement ACS, its recovery-rate objective, or its proof; our predictor is not a calibrated deployment-time bound and the screen has no formal guarantee. V5 uses fresh trace panels and validation-only promotion; see `docs/SAFETY_SHIELD_V5.md`.

### Multiple-test risk control and post-selection calibration

15. **Angelopoulos et al. (2022), Learn then Test: Calibrating Predictive Algorithms to Achieve Risk Control**, [arXiv 2110.01052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01052). Hugging Face search/info returned the paper metadata, but `hf papers read 2110.01052` reported that the item was not available on the Hub; the original arXiv HTML/PDF was retrieved and §§1.1, 2.1--2.3 were read. LTT tests a finite family of risk hypotheses with valid concentration-based p-values and a family-wise-error-controlling procedure; unlike conformal risk control, it does not require risk to be monotone in the post-processing parameter. The theorem still needs valid p-values for the target risk. For a closed-loop controller, whole episodes—not correlated time steps—could be bounded-loss units if episode traces are independent draws from a prespecified target distribution and the candidate family is fixed. A separate risk-control panel and a fresh test would be needed; the current v3--v5 promotion grids do not implement LTT and have no such guarantee.

16. **Du (2025 arXiv preprint), Optimistic Feasible Search for Closed-Loop Fair Threshold Decision-Making**, [arXiv 2512.22313](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.22313). Retrieved with `hf papers read 2512.22313 --format human`; the abstract, closed-loop method/assumptions, benchmarks and conclusion were consulted. OFS studies a one-dimensional threshold bandit and its high-probability argument assumes stable contractive dynamics with bounded noise. This is relevant evidence that decisions can change future data, but it is not a controller baseline for our 42-action media system: the feedback process, constraints and required contraction assumptions differ, and we do not claim its results or guarantees transfer.
