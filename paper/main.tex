\begin{abstract}
Real-time media adaptation balances quality, delay and loss under changing network conditions. RLCD combines a Double DQN media policy, a separately fitted and calibrated next-interval safety predictor, and deterministic fallback. Evaluation labels rejected proposals separately from executed actions, avoiding attribution of fallback successes to calibrated confidence. Across nine synthetic studies, the initial \EvidenceNumSeeds{}-seed evaluation spans \EvidenceNumEpisodes{} episodes and \EvidenceNumSteps{} intervals. Gating reduces stress violations relative to its identical ungated policy, but does not dominate deterministic controllers. Validation-locked safety-refit, threshold and policy-randomization follow-ups do not justify changing the reference. Action-wise screening and ID selected-action recalibration improve nominal outcomes, while stress confidence remains optimistic; a disagreement guard intervenes rarely. A subsequent causal wire-budget screen reduces stress violations by 2.862 percentage points on 8,800 fresh episodes (conditional model-seed interval $[-3.724,-1.986]$), but lowers ID and stress QoE by 0.104 and 0.133 reward units. It is rejected by the frozen validation QoE constraint. Removing probability/disagreement eligibility from this envelope produces no resolved violation disadvantage, questioning confidence's incremental benefit under this particular guard. A delay-evidence follow-up recovers 1.160 burst-loss reward units relative to the loss-responsive envelope with unchanged observed violations, but still fails nominal-quality promotion. Its matched confidence ablation shows a small stress-risk regression from adding eligibility. A tenth study uses a different model class on a bitrate-only testbed with a corrected bandwidth estimator: a small head (\TypedParameters{} parameters) answers one typed six-way question per second and is trained on strictly proper scoring rules with labels from counterfactual simulator rollouts. Multiplying its probabilities by a fixed payoff table raises utility on the four training families by \TypedAuditIdBayesVsTrain{} over the fixed estimate ratio selected on those families (family-level interval \TypedAuditIdBayesVsTrainT{}). On seven held-out families the mean gain is \TypedAuditOodBayesVsTrain{}, but its family-level interval \TypedAuditOodBayesVsTrainT{} includes zero and confidence is over-optimistic (ECE \TypedAuditOodBayesEce{}). After training on thirteen broader families, one evaluation on twelve untouched families gives \TypedBroadTestOodBayesVsTrain{} \TypedBroadTestOodBayesVsTrainT{}, an ECE of \TypedBroadTestOodBayesEce{} and fewer unsafe intervals; of three criteria fixed in advance only the constraint criterion is met. An exact-gradient control and an untyped regression match the head throughout, so the gain is not attributable to the scoring-rule policy gradient. No rollout information reaches a controller at run time, and none is available on a real network. An eleventh study adds no model. A tuned reactive rule over the same options gains at most \TypedRuleValRuleVsFixed{} over the fixed ratio, so the comparator was not weak. A registered replication on \TypedRepFamilies{} families drawn from a stated population meets \TypedRepCriteriaMet{} of \TypedRepCriteria{} criteria: the gain is \TypedRepGain{} \TypedRepGainT{}, \TypedRepOracleShare\% of the hindsight oracle's and not established against the best fixed ratio in hindsight (\TypedRepGainVsHindsight{} \TypedRepGainVsHindsightT{}), and ECE is \TypedRepEce{}. With one randomised option per decision in place of rollouts, a factual learner's in-distribution gain is established from \TypedFactIdentifiedHours{} hours of link time and reaches \TypedFactFourGain{} at \TypedFactFourHours{} hours, where rollout labels need \TypedFactOneHours{} hours for \TypedFactOneControlGain{}; a within-session crossover does not identify a single link's best ratio. \end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Interactive media is sensitive not only to sustained throughput but also to short queueing excursions, late frames, burst loss and missing feedback. Increasing source bitrate can improve visual quality while increasing queueing delay. Forward error correction (FEC) can recover erasures while consuming bandwidth that might itself cause congestion. Low-latency media modes trade coding efficiency or quality for timeliness. These coupled decisions make learned adaptation attractive, but also make an unqualified reward-maximizing policy difficult to trust when network conditions change.

Requirements for real-time congestion control include low delay, stable behavior, rapid adaptation and appropriate handling of feedback loss~\cite{rfc8836}. Prior learned congestion-control work identifies generalization, safety and fairness as significant deployment challenges~\cite{jay2019aurora}. A policy's preference for an action does not resolve those challenges: Q-values describe expected return under the learned model, not the probability that the action meets an operational latency or loss constraint. A softmax over Q-values therefore has no intrinsic interpretation as safety confidence.

We investigate a different interface: the controller proposes a joint media action and separately reports an estimated probability that this action will satisfy a precisely defined next-interval constraint. A post-hoc calibrator is fitted on disjoint episodes: in-distribution (ID) only in v1, and a declared ID/stress mixture in the stress-refit variants. A deterministic fallback is used when confidence is low, feedback is unusable, or a separate telemetry-support guard rejects the observation. We call this implemented controller \emph{RLCD}; the name is a project identifier, not a claim to reproduce a previously established algorithm. Section~\ref{sec:typed} separately studies a typed decision head trained with an unrelated recipe that the typed-decision literature calls reinforcement learning for calibrated decisions; that section keeps the two uses of the abbreviation apart.

The central question is not whether adding a confidence score makes RL safe. It is whether an empirically calibrated action-safety estimate improves a deployed risk--quality tradeoff, and whether that improvement persists beyond the calibration distribution. We make four contributions: (i) an explicit proposal-level confidence target and causal controller interface; (ii) a reproducible evaluation protocol that distinguishes rejected-proposal outcomes from executed fallback outcomes; (iii) an open experimental implementation with paired-seed comparisons, proper scoring rules, fallback diagnostics and reproducible negative findings; and (iv) a bitrate-only typed-decision study that corrects an estimator artifact in its own baseline, trains a probabilistic choice head on counterfactual rollout labels, audits it by network family, and tests it once on untouched families against criteria fixed in advance. We do not introduce a new calibration estimator or prove an invariant safety property.

\section{Related work and scope}
\paragraph{Learned and deterministic congestion control.}
Aurora studies Internet congestion control through deep reinforcement learning and emphasizes adoption challenges beyond average reward~\cite{jay2019aurora}. We use a small Double DQN~\cite{hasselt2016double} over a joint bitrate/FEC/media action space; we do not reproduce Aurora's architecture or results. Our GCC-like baseline is inspired by the delay-overuse and loss-response mechanisms in the historical GCC draft~\cite{holmer2016gcc}, but omits production libwebrtc's packet estimators and probe machinery. It must not be interpreted as a production GCC benchmark.

\paragraph{Calibration and selective prediction.}
Guo et al. study held-out post-hoc probability scaling~\cite{guo2017calibration}; ensembles offer a practical predictive-uncertainty construction~\cite{lakshminarayanan2017ensembles}. Ovadia et al. show that calibration under distribution shift remains problematic~\cite{ovadia2019shift}. These works motivate separate fitting and evaluation of safety probabilities, rather than treating the calibration transformation as a guarantee. SelectiveNet jointly optimizes prediction and rejection~\cite{geifman2019selectivenet}. RLCD instead fits an action-safety predictor separately from its policy, calibrates it post hoc, and deploys a stateful fallback. Its probability-only risk--coverage curves are descriptive diagnostics, not a reproduction of SelectiveNet.

\paragraph{Randomized training coverage.}
Domain randomization exposes a model to varied simulated conditions rather than a single nominal environment~\cite{tobin2017randomization}. Tobin et al. study visual object localization and physical robotic transfer; our v2 use randomizes network-stress supervision for a safety predictor. Tiboni et al. study adaptive entropy-maximizing distributions for robotic sim-to-real transfer~\cite{tiboni2023doraemon}; v4 does not implement their method or inherit its evidence, but tests one fixed policy-training mixture. All v4 stress mechanisms occur during candidate policy training, so fresh v4 traces measure new realizations rather than unseen-mechanism generalization.

\paragraph{Finite-sample risk control and threshold selection.}
Conformal risk control bounds expected bounded losses under exchangeability and a monotone-risk condition; extensions relax exchangeability only under their own shift assumptions~\cite{angelopoulos2022conformalrisk} and~\cite{farinhas2023nonexchangeable}. Learn-then-Test instead frames a finite candidate family as risk hypotheses and combines finite-sample valid p-values with family-wise error control, without requiring monotone risk~\cite{angelopoulos2022learntest}. It could in principle screen a prespecified family of closed-loop controllers if full-episode losses were bounded and calibration episodes were independent draws from a declared target distribution. We do not apply it here: the existing threshold study uses empirical validation for promotion, not risk-control p-values or an independent target-distribution calibration panel; per-step outcomes are serially dependent and the scenario panel is a fixed heterogeneous design. We therefore make no conformal, distribution-free or finite-sample safety-guarantee claim.

\paragraph{Typed decision models, proper scoring and rollout imitation.}
Typed decision models return a probability for each option of a schema-constrained question instead of generated text, and are positioned as fast first-pass deciders whose confidence can be thresholded~\cite{laya2026modelcard}\cite{barbosa2026jev}. The Laya model card describes training by REINFORCE over noisy reported logits with strictly proper scoring rewards; Jev's training is unpublished, and the Laya card itself reports that its raw probabilities are over-confident until a temperature is refitted. Strictly proper scoring rules are maximised in expectation only by the true distribution~\cite{gneiting2007scoring}, which is the property that recipe relies on; REINFORCE supplies the estimator~\cite{williams1992reinforce}. Study~10 applies the recipe to a six-way bitrate question. Its labels come from one-step rollouts of a base policy, so the head imitates a policy-improvement step as in classification-based policy iteration~\cite{lagoudakis2003classification}, and its training states are aggregated from the learner's own trajectories~\cite{ross2011dagger}. Acting on calibrated class probabilities through a payoff matrix is standard cost-sensitive decision making~\cite{elkan2001cost}. We reuse these ideas; we do not reproduce Jev or Laya, and we run neither model.

\paragraph{Runtime assurance and chance-constrained action screens.}
Formal shielding can synthesize interventions from temporal-logic specifications and an appropriate abstraction of environment dynamics~\cite{alshiekh2017shielding}. Adaptive Chance-Constraint Safeguards (ACS) instead evaluates actions against a chance constraint and projects into a feasible set, coupled to a learned recovery policy and explicit theoretical assumptions~\cite{chen2023acs}. V5 tests a much narrower empirical screen: among the five highest-Q actions, choose the first whose existing calibrated one-step probability clears the fixed threshold; otherwise use the deterministic fallback. It implements neither ACS's recovery objective nor its proof. Kenton et al. report mixed results for ensemble-based catastrophe avoidance across gridworld and CoinRun, while finding that ensemble uncertainty can help predict near-term risk in some settings~\cite{kenton2019generalizing}. This motivates our V7 disagreement-abstention ablation only: it does not establish OOD detection or transfer. RLCD's learned risk estimate, disagreement score and telemetry support distance do not establish a winning region, recoverability, or temporal invariant. A deterministic fallback is reproducible, but determinism alone does not make it safe; in particular, a controller cannot recover bandwidth during a complete outage.

\section{Problem formulation and simulator}
\subsection{Causal observations and joint media actions}
At each interval of length $\Delta=0.1$ s, the controller receives ten telemetry features: acknowledged wire throughput, RTT, raw loss, jitter, RTT change, the last locally selected bitrate, FEC level and media mode, feedback age, and a validity bit. Feedback becomes available only after the interval that generated it; configured extra delay and feedback gaps retain stale measurements. The controller never receives current capacity, the queue state, scenario identity, or future trace samples. Fixed feature scales and clipping are documented in the artifact.

The action is $a_t=(b_t,f_t,m_t)$, where source bitrate $b_t$ belongs to $\{0.15,0.3,0.6,1,1.6,2.5,4\}$ Mbps, FEC overhead $f_t$ belongs to $\{0,0.1,0.25\}$, and $m_t$ selects normal or low-latency mode. Thus there are 42 actions with wire rate $w_t=b_t(1+f_t)$. Low-latency mode uses an 8 ms coding-delay proxy and quality multiplier 0.82, versus 25 ms and multiplier 1 for normal mode. These values are synthetic modeling assumptions, not codec measurements.

\subsection{Network and media transition}
The trace specifies bottleneck capacity $C_t$, base RTT, exogenous erasure probability, jitter, feedback availability and burst/buffer modifiers. Given queue occupancy $Q_t$ Mbit and buffer limit $B_t$, arrivals and fluid service are
\begin{align}
A_t &= w_t\Delta, & S_t &= \min(Q_t+A_t,C_t\Delta), \\
D_t &= \max(0,Q_t+A_t-S_t-B_t), & Q_{t+1}&=Q_t+A_t-S_t-D_t.
\end{align}
Tail-drop loss is approximated by $\min(1,D_t/A_t)$ and composed with exogenous erasures. For burst intensity $z_t$, the idealized recoverable fraction is
\begin{equation}
\rho_t=0.85(1-0.7z_t)\frac{f_t}{1+f_t},\qquad
\ell_t^{\mathrm{res}}=\frac{\max(0,p_t^{\mathrm{loss}}-\rho_t)}{1-\rho_t}.
\end{equation}
FEC therefore adds both wire load and a $20f_t$ ms coding-delay proxy. At zero capacity, residual loss and deadline misses are forced to one. The model does not implement erasure-code blocks or packet identities.

Virtual queue delay is $1000(Q_t+Q_{t+1})/[2\max(C_t,0.01)]$ ms. Virtual media latency $L_t$ adds half the base RTT, jitter, coding delay and this queue delay. A uniform completion-time approximation around $L_t$ yields a deadline-miss fraction
\begin{equation}
v_t=\operatorname{clip}\left(\tfrac12+\frac{L_t-L_{\mathrm{deadline}}}{1000\Delta},0,1\right).
\end{equation}
The 0.01 Mbps denominator floor makes stalled-interval penalties finite. Consequently, large outage latency values are \emph{virtual delay penalties, not observed packet delivery times}. Timely-goodput accounting is bounded by fluid service and discounts residual loss and deadline misses.

\subsection{Reward and the safety event}
With $u(b,m)=\log(1+b/0.15)$ multiplied by the mode-quality factor, the default reward is
\begin{align}
r_t={}&u(b_t,m_t)(1-\ell_t^{\mathrm{res}})(1-v_t)
 -0.8\min(L_t/150,10)\nonumber \\
 &-5\ell_t^{\mathrm{res}}-2v_t-0.15\left|\log(b_t/b_{t-1})\right|.
\end{align}
QoE here denotes this synthetic reward, not MOS or VMAF. Reward weights are configurable and shared by all methods. We separately define the binary event
\begin{equation}\label{eq:safety}
Y_t(a)=\mathbf{1}\left\{L_t(a)\leq150\;\mathrm{ms},\;\ell_t^{\mathrm{res}}(a)\leq0.05,\;C_t>0\right\}.
\end{equation}
The event is not reward positivity, zero deadline misses, action optimality or full-episode safety. Capacity appears only in outcome labeling, never in the policy or confidence features. The thresholds are experimental design choices, not requirements asserted by RFC 8836.

\section{RLCD controller}
\subsection{Policy training}
A one-hidden-layer ReLU MLP estimates action values. Double DQN separates online action selection from target-network evaluation~\cite{hasselt2016double}:
\begin{equation}
y_t=r_t+\gamma(1-d_t)Q_{\theta^-}\left(o_{t+1},\arg\max_a Q_{\theta}(o_{t+1},a)\right),
\end{equation}
where $d_t$ is terminal status. Training uses uniform replay, Huber loss, Adam, a norm-10 gradient cap, and periodically copied target weights. Exploration decays to its configured floor. The final checkpoint is used without test-driven early stopping. The policy-training objective remains expected discounted reward; the runtime gate is a separate empirical intervention, not an exact solution to a constrained MDP.

\subsection{Action-safety estimator and calibration}
The greedy proposal is $a_t^*=\arg\max_aQ_\theta(o_t,a)$. Safety features concatenate telemetry with the candidate bitrate, redundancy, media mode and a wire-rate/acknowledged-throughput ratio. Standardization uses safety-fit data only. Three separately initialized classifiers are trained with binary cross-entropy on whole-episode bootstrap resamples, and their sigmoid probabilities are averaged to obtain $p_t$. Model disagreement is logged but is not treated as a Bayesian credible interval.

In v1, safety fitting uses independent ID episodes with a predeclared repeating mixture of RL, conservative and GCC-like behavior; random-action augmentation adds action coverage. Calibration uses new ID trace seeds and only labels the RL proposal on that trajectory mixture. Monotone Platt scaling fits
\begin{equation}
q_t=\sigma\left(\exp(\alpha)\operatorname{logit}(p_t)+\beta\right)
\end{equation}
by held-out binary negative log likelihood (NLL), with the underlying models frozen. Temperature scaling fixes $\beta=0$. Single-class calibration sets produce a recorded smoothed prior instead of an ill-conditioned fit; such a result is an insufficient-data warning, not evidence of successful calibration.

The operational interpretation of $q_t$ is an estimate of $P(Y_t(a_t^*)=1\mid o_t,a_t^*)$. It does not estimate the correctness of the subsequently substituted fallback action. Even within ID mechanisms, the deployed gate changes the distribution of visited states relative to calibration trajectories; this occupancy shift is a limitation to be measured rather than assumed away.

\subsection{Support rejection, hysteresis and fallback}
The support score is the RMS standardized distance of the ten unclipped telemetry features from their safety-fit mean. Its cutoff is the safety-fit 99.5th percentile. This deliberately simple heuristic is separate from the calibrated probability: it neither inspects the scenario label nor multiplies $q_t$ by an arbitrary penalty.

The default gate rejects when $q_t<0.90$, feedback is invalid or older than 0.5 s, or telemetry exceeds the support cutoff. Nonfinite telemetry bypasses neural inference. Once fallback begins, it lasts at least three decisions and requires $q_t\geq0.93$ to release. These hold/release rules reduce switching chatter but do not establish an invariant. Algorithm~1 states the decision interface.

V1's conservative fallback maintains a wire-rate budget and an RTT-excess estimate relative to the observed minimum. Queueing, increasing delay or substantial loss causes multiplicative backoff capped by acknowledged throughput; otherwise it probes additively. A persistent budget allows progress across discrete bitrate rungs despite application-limited acknowledgments. It favors low-latency mode and modest FEC only outside congestion, selecting a minimum-wire action if the budget cannot accommodate another rung. Outages and base-delay changes can still violate Eq.~\ref{eq:safety} under fallback.

\section{Experimental design}
\subsection{Questions, baselines and completed studies}
We ask whether gating improves its identical ungated policy, whether probability scaling improves same-state proper scores, which components explain any gains, and whether deterministic controllers remain stronger. Baselines include continuous conservative, throughput/queue/loss heuristic, GCC-like control and the exact ungated Double DQN checkpoint. V1 additionally tests raw scores, no support rejection, no hysteresis and a single calibrated predictor. Physical/FEC and temperature sweeps exist only at smoke scale and do not support full-budget conclusions.

Four ID trace families provide steady, stepped and ramped capacity and correlated Wi-Fi-like variation. Seven stress families provide severe capacity collapse, burst erasures, bufferbloat, handover, outage, missing feedback and capacity expansion. V1 policy, safety fitting and calibration use only ID families, so the seven stress families are mechanism-held-out for v1. The completed scale-up partly overlaps the earlier pilot's trace seeds and is descriptive rather than untouched confirmatory evidence.

\subsection{V2 safety-refit protocol and selection lock}
V1 outcomes were inspected before designing v2; the original panel is therefore development evidence. The first three model seeds, 11, 22 and 33, are fixed in advance. Their policy weights, simulator, reward, action space and gate thresholds are unchanged. Each newly trained safety ensemble uses 40 fitting episodes and 40 disjoint calibration episodes. A uniform random-action candidate supplements the greedy proposal during risk fitting; duplicate candidates are labeled once. Behavior alternates between RL, conservative and GCC-like trajectories, with 15\% random executed actions during fitting only. Calibration still labels only greedy proposals.

Three candidates isolate plausible changes. \textbf{ID refit} uses ID-only episodes with the richer action-label coverage. \textbf{Stress + safe} uses the same episode/optimizer budget, replacing half the fitting and calibration episodes with randomized stress traces. \textbf{Stress + GCC} shares exactly those learned safety weights and calibrator, but replaces the conservative fallback with the existing deterministic GCC-like controller. Thus the latter contrast changes fallback composition, not the neural policy or probability estimator. Episode budgets are matched; deduplication causes small differences in total label counts, recorded in each checkpoint.

Randomized training traces have five random change points. Per segment, capacity is log-uniform on $[0.12,7]$ Mbps, base RTT uniform on $[25,230]$ ms, baseline erasure probability uniform on $[0.001,0.025]$, and buffer multiplier uniformly selected from $\{1,2,4,8\}$. A two-state burst process enters with probability 0.025 and exits with probability 0.2 per interval; its episode-level erasure severity is uniform on $[0.06,0.4]$. Absolute Gaussian jitter has an episode-level scale drawn uniformly from 1--15 ms. Half the traces contain a randomly located 3--15-interval feedback gap, clipped at the episode boundary. These are training generators, not replays of evaluation traces. The original seven \texttt{ood} families are called the \emph{stress panel} for v2: many of their mechanisms now overlap safety-model training support. Fresh realizations do not imply fresh mechanisms.

Risk fitting, calibration and validation use independent hashed namespaces \texttt{risk\_v2}, \texttt{calibration\_v2} and \texttt{validation}. Validation uses seeds 501--502 across all eleven families. A candidate is feasible if its ID QoE is at least v1 minus 0.08, its ID violation rate at most v1 plus 0.002, and its stress violation rate no worse than v1. Among feasible candidates, maximize
\[
J=\tfrac12(\overline r_{\mathrm{ID}}+\overline r_{\mathrm{stress}})-5\overline v_{\mathrm{stress}},
\]
requiring $J-J_{\mathrm{v1}}>0.01$; otherwise retain v1. All candidates and tolerances were recorded before evaluation. The complete ranking and selection are persisted before the fresh test seeds 1501--1504 are evaluated in the separate \texttt{test} namespace. No reselection follows fresh-test outcomes. The selected controller is denoted RLCD v2 below; explicit v1 and refit controls remain in the final matrix.

\subsection{Labels, metrics and statistical scope}
An evaluation-only simulator preview labels each proposed action before execution changes the queue, including rejected proposals. Proposal confidence never uses the executed fallback's outcome as its label. The runtime controller cannot call preview or inspect latent capacity. We report synthetic QoE, virtual-latency tails, raw/residual loss, deadline misses, goodput, action stability and fallback rate/reasons/harm. Probability diagnostics include NLL, Brier score, ten-bin ECE, bin counts and selective risk; zero accepted actions yield undefined selective risk, not zero risk.

Comparisons average the shared trace panel within each model seed and bootstrap model-seed means. Paired contrasts subtract matched model/trace/scenario outcomes before resampling. These conditional intervals omit uncertainty over new network populations; deterministic baselines may have degenerate intervals. Three model seeds support exploratory follow-up estimates only, not strong multiple-comparison-adjusted significance claims. For probability attribution, v2 additionally replays the \emph{same ungated policy states and labels} through every predictor, eliminating cross-controller occupancy differences in that benchmark. Raw versus calibrated scores on each main controller's own trajectory remain a separate diagnostic.

\section{V5 action-wise calibrated safety screen}
\paragraph{Frozen protocol.}
The v2 refit and v4 randomized policy were not promoted, and v3 retained the original 0.90 gate. V5 therefore freezes v1 policy, safety-ensemble and calibrator weights and tests only an action-selection change motivated by chance-constrained screening~\cite{chen2023acs}. At each valid observation, the controller ranks the 42 joint actions by the original Q values and checks the top five in order. It executes the first candidate whose calibrated one-step safety probability is at least 0.90 and whose state-support score is within the source checkpoint's limit; otherwise, including invalid/stale telemetry, it uses the existing deterministic fallback. No predictor or policy is retrained. The calibration set was collected for the original proposal distribution, so screening alternatives may introduce selection bias; the intervention is empirical, not a formal shield.

The ten source-policy seeds are evaluated on ten validation and ten disjoint final trace seeds over the same eleven synthetic scenarios. Validation alone can promote the shield only if the ID QoE loss is at most 0.03, ID violation increase at most 0.01, aggregate stress violation increase at most 0.02, every stress-family increase at most 0.03, and the fixed score gain exceeds 0.005. Validation selected the shield, with score gain 0.02582; the selection and validation-manifest hash were saved before final traces were created. All family mechanisms are already known; the final panel measures new realizations, not unseen-mechanism generalization. \path{docs/SAFETY_SHIELD_V5.md} records the full protocol, including the excluded development smoke probe.

\section{V7 ensemble-disagreement abstention}
V6's fresh OOD audit found that calibrated confidence remained optimistic for screen-selected actions. Since the safety predictor is an episode-bootstrap ensemble and its disagreement was previously logged but unused by the action screen, V7 tests whether this signal is useful as an additional abstention criterion. The hypothesis is intentionally narrow and informed by prior results; it is not a confirmatory real-network claim.

The V7 baseline and candidate use the same V6 selected-action-calibrated checkpoints, policy, safety ensemble, calibrator, top-five Q ranking, 0.90 calibrated-probability threshold, telemetry support check and deterministic fallback. The candidate accepts a probability-eligible action only when the ensemble standard deviation of its raw safety probabilities is at most 0.05; if every probability-eligible candidate exceeds this cutoff, it falls back with reason \texttt{uncertainty\_abstention}. The cutoff is fixed at five probability points in the preregistered JSON; it is not a calibrated lower confidence bound and does not alter the estimated safety probability. Thus the V6-to-V7 comparison changes only inference-time action screening, not training or calibration.

The ten frozen model seeds are evaluated on ten validation trace seeds (10701--10710) and a disjoint ten-seed fresh test panel (11701--11710), across all eleven known scenario families. Validation compares V7 against the V6 action screen under the same promotion bounds and score rule; the fresh panel is opened only after this decision is persisted. Since the stress families are known and V7 is an adaptive follow-up, results measure new realizations of the synthetic scenarios, not unseen-mechanism generalization. Step-wise disagreement and abstention diagnostics are descriptive and do not imply an episode-level guarantee.

Validation selected V7 by a score gain of 0.00507, only marginally above the preregistered 0.005 threshold. On the 8,800-episode fresh test panel, the paired ID changes versus V6 were negligible: QoE $+0.00043$ (model-seed interval $[-0.00004,0.00134]$) and violation rate $-0.003$ percentage points (interval $[-0.018,0.009]$). This latter interval narrowly excludes zero under the fixed-panel model-seed bootstrap, but the absolute effect is small. The cutoff triggered on only 0.24\% of stress steps (0.04\% ID), limiting its practical impact. The V7 OOD means (QoE 1.071, violations 10.51\%) remain worse than GCC-like control (1.119, 7.21\%). These are conditional descriptive effects, not a safety or real-network guarantee.

\section{V8 causal wire-budget screening}
\subsection{Frozen controller and matched ablation}
V7's disagreement criterion rarely activates. V8 tests a complementary signal available without inspecting latent capacity: the existing conservative fallback's causal, stateful wire-rate budget. Both screens retain the same V6-derived policy, safety ensemble, selected-action calibrator, top-five ranking, probability threshold, disagreement cutoff, feedback/support guards and fallback. Let $B_t$ be the shadow fallback's budget after its single update at decision $t$, and $s_t(a)$ the raw ensemble standard deviation. The V8 eligibility set is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{A}_t &= \operatorname{Top5}(Q(o_t,\cdot)),\\
\mathcal{C}_t &= \{a\in\mathcal{A}_t:
 q_t(a)\geq 0.90,\ s_t(a)\leq 0.05,\ b(a)(1+f(a))\leq B_t\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The controller executes the highest-Q member, or the deterministic fallback when this set is empty. If actions eligible under both probability and disagreement criteria exist but all exceed the budget, the distinct reason is \texttt{budget\_abstention}. FEC overhead participates in the constraint; neither the budget nor rejection changes the reported probability.

The default budget starts at 0.6 Mbps. With usable feedback, RTT excess above its running minimum greater than 35 ms, loss above 0.06, or RTT trend above 15 ms causes backoff to $\max(0.15,\min(0.7B_{t-1},0.8T_t))$, where $T_t$ is acknowledged wire throughput. Otherwise the budget probes by $0.5\Delta$ Mbps, capped at the largest configured wire rate. Missing/stale/nonfinite feedback invokes the existing halving rule. Persistent probing avoids treating application-limited acknowledgments as capacity. These pre-existing fallback dynamics are not fitted or changed in V8. The budget is a heuristic envelope, not a capacity lower bound, invariant region or safety certificate.

A test-only \texttt{budget\_only} ablation removes probability/disagreement eligibility but retains the same Q ranking, feedback/support guards, envelope and fallback. Scores are still computed for diagnostics; this ablation is neither model-free nor a runtime-cost comparison. It tests what probability-based eligibility adds after a causal envelope is present, and cannot become a post-test promotion choice.

\section{Study 10: typed bitrate decisions trained on proper scoring rules}
\label{sec:typed}
The nine studies above gate a value-based policy with a separately fitted safety probability. This study asks a narrower question with a different model class. Can a small head that answers one typed question, and reports a calibrated probability for every option, improve a bitrate-only controller over a tuned deterministic rule?

\subsection{Typed decisions and the second meaning of RLCD}
Typed decision models such as Jev and Laya answer a schema-constrained question (a choice among listed options, an ordinal score or a yes/no) with a probability per option in one forward pass, so that software can act on the answer above a chosen confidence~\cite{laya2026modelcard}\cite{barbosa2026jev}. Their training recipe is called \emph{reinforcement learning for calibrated decisions}, which is also abbreviated RLCD and is unrelated to this project's name. Jev's recipe is unpublished. We follow the public description on the Laya model card: the policy reports a distribution, exploration adds zero-mean Gaussian noise to the logits, the reward is a strictly proper scoring rule, and updates are REINFORCE with a group-mean baseline. In this section \emph{typed head} names the model and \emph{proper-score RLCD} names the recipe; ``RLCD'' alone keeps its earlier meaning. No pretrained Jev or Laya weights are used or evaluated.

\subsection{Controller interface and a corrected estimator}
The typed head controls bitrate only. FEC is off and the media mode is fixed. Every method requests a cap $c_t=\rho_t\,\hat B_t$, where $\hat B_t$ is a GCC-like bandwidth estimate and $\rho_t$ is one of six ratios $\{0.60,0.70,0.80,0.90,1.00,1.05\}$. A shared governor then applies the same hard rules to every method: increases wait 1.8\,s after the previous change, matching a modelled 1.5\,s encoder response to higher caps; decreases are immediate; congestion evidence (loss of at least 10\%, RTT 70\,ms above its floor, or a sharp delay rise) cuts the cap to 0.7 of its current value and blocks increases for 1.8\,s; stale feedback forces the minimum rate. Utility is the simulator's delivered QoE minus the switch penalty on the requested cap.

An earlier attempt at this interface compared a learned ratio against a fixed $0.85\,\hat B_t$ fallback and found no gain. Table~\ref{tab:typed-diagnosis} shows why that null result was uninformative. The legacy GCC-like budget grows whenever delay and loss are low, however little the sender delivers, so a sender below capacity drives $\hat B_t$ to the ladder maximum and the ratio silently becomes an absolute cap. Under that estimator the best fixed ratio on ID tune traces is the lowest option, \TypedDiagLegacyBest{}, with utility \TypedDiagLegacyBestUtility{} against \TypedDiagLegacyFallbackUtility{} for the 0.85 fallback: the reference was untuned and the option set was truncated at its optimum. GCC bounds its delay-based estimate by 1.5 times the measured incoming rate~\cite{holmer2016gcc}. We add only that bound. Ratios below about 0.75 then stall the estimate, the best fixed ratio becomes interior (\TypedDiagAckedBest{}), and a hindsight oracle shows headroom that depends on context (\TypedDiagAckedOracleUtility{} against \TypedDiagAckedBestUtility{}). The corrected estimator is still a simplified model, not libwebrtc.

\subsection{Question, hindsight labels and dataset aggregation}
Once per second the head receives a 119-dimensional causal state: four ordered snapshots spanning three seconds of thirteen telemetry fields with presence masks, plus eleven long-memory summaries such as the delivered rate at the last congestion onset, the time since congestion and since the last cap change, and rates relative to $\hat B_t$. No clock, scenario identity, capacity or future outcome is an input. The typed question is: \emph{which ratio gives the highest mean utility over the next five seconds if it is held for three seconds and the base ratio runs afterwards?}

The label comes from the simulator. At each decision the episode is forked once per option and replayed on the true future trace under the shared governor, giving a value $Q_t(a)$ for every option. The label is $\arg\max_a Q_t(a)$, except that the base ratio wins unless another option beats it by 0.02. These are hindsight labels for fitting and evaluation only; no controller receives them. The base ratio is the best fixed ratio on 64 ID tune episodes, here \TypedBaseRatio{}. Imitating a one-step rollout improvement of a base policy is classification-based policy iteration~\cite{lagoudakis2003classification}.

Training states must come from the trajectories the learned controller itself produces. Round one mixes the base ratio with uniformly random options. Each later round runs the current typed policy with 20\% random options, labels every visited decision, and refits on all rounds together~\cite{ross2011dagger}. Each model seed aggregates \TypedRounds{} rounds of 800 ID episodes, \TypedTrainRows{} labelled decisions.

\subsection{Proper-score RLCD and its exact-gradient control}
The head is one hidden layer of 64 units with \TypedParameters{} parameters. For logits $z$ and label $y$, each of $G=8$ noisy reports $z+\epsilon_g$, $\epsilon_g\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2 I)$ with $\sigma=0.2$, earns
\begin{equation}
R_g=\log p_y+\kappa\,\frac{p_y}{\lVert p\rVert_2},\qquad p=\mathrm{softmax}(z+\epsilon_g),\quad \kappa=0.5 .
\end{equation}
The logarithmic and spherical scores are strictly proper, so expected reward is maximised only by the true conditional distribution of the label~\cite{gneiting2007scoring}. The update follows $\hat g=\frac{1}{G}\sum_g (R_g-b_g)\,\epsilon_g/\sigma^2$ with a leave-one-out group baseline $b_g$~\cite{williams1992reinforce}. In expectation $\hat g$ is the gradient of the Gaussian-smoothed score, so the estimator is a noisy form of exact gradient ascent on the same objective. An otherwise identical head trained with the exact gradient measures what that noise costs. One temperature per head is then fitted on 200 held-out ID episodes run by the deployed rule~\cite{guo2017calibration}.

\subsection{Three ways to act on the answer}
\emph{Most probable} executes $\arg\max_a p_a$ with no guard. \emph{Confidence-gated} executes it only when its calibrated probability reaches a threshold chosen on the calibration episodes, and otherwise runs the base ratio; this is the usage pattern advertised for typed decision models. \emph{Payoff-weighted} executes $\arg\max_a \sum_b p_b M_{ab}$, where $M_{ab}$ is the mean advantage over the base of running option $a$ when option $b$ turned out best, measured on the calibration episodes. The base row of $M$ is zero, so this rule returns to the base by itself whenever no option has positive expected advantage; it is the standard cost-sensitive use of calibrated class probabilities~\cite{elkan2001cost}. The gated and payoff-weighted rules also return to the base on stale telemetry or when the standardised state lies beyond the 99.5th percentile of training support. An untyped baseline regresses the advantage of every option with the same network size and data, and executes its argmax under the same two guards.

\subsection{From six probabilities to a requested bitrate}
\label{sec:typed-runtime}
All headline numbers use the payoff-weighted rule. Its run-time path is short. Once per second (i) the 119 inputs are standardised with the training mean and scale and clipped to $\pm12$; (ii) one forward pass gives logits $z$ and probabilities $p=\mathrm{softmax}(z/T)$ with the frozen temperature $T$; (iii) the expected advantage of each option is $e=Mp$ and the proposal is $a^\star=\arg\max_a e_a$; (iv) the base ratio runs instead if $a^\star$ is the base option, if $e_{a^\star}\le 0$, if feedback is invalid or older than 500\,ms, or if the root-mean-square standardised input exceeds the support limit. The selected ratio $\rho$ is held until the next decision. Every 100\,ms the shared governor turns $\rho\,\hat B_t$ into the requested cap, clips it to the 150\,kbps floor and to $\min(4\,\mathrm{Mbps},\,1.05\,\hat B_t)$, and applies the hard rules described above. The weights, the standardisation, $T$, $M$, the gate threshold and the support limit are fitted on ID episodes and frozen before the test panel is evaluated.

``Weighting by measured payoffs'' therefore means multiplying the reported probabilities by a constant $6\times6$ table (Table~\ref{tab:typed-payoff}). Entry $M_{ab}$ averages, over the ID calibration decisions whose hindsight label was $b$, the rollout value of option $a$ minus that of the base. The table is built from simulator rollouts, so like the labels it is counterfactual information at fitting time. At run time it is a constant: no rollout, forked simulator state, future trace sample or label reaches a controller. Two checks support this beyond reading the code. Recomputing steps (iii) and (iv) from the recorded probabilities and the frozen table reproduces \TypedAuditRuleAgreement\% of the options that ran on the test panel. Replaying all \TypedAuditReplayEpisodes{} learned test episodes with hindsight rollouts switched off reproduces every frozen episode outcome, with maximum absolute difference \TypedAuditReplayDifference{}. Hindsight rollouts enter the training labels, the table $M$, the calibration fits, the oracle rows and the scoring of accuracy and calibration, and nothing else.

\subsection{Protocol}
All fitting, calibration and tuning use the four ID families. The estimator bound, the option set and the rollout horizon were settled from a fixed-ratio and oracle sweep before any model was trained; that sweep used seeds disjoint from every later split but covered all eleven families, so the held-out families informed those three choices. The long-memory summaries, the payoff-weighted rule, the rollout tail, the network width and the number of aggregation rounds were then developed on one model seed using the ID validation panel (16 trace seeds per family). The configuration was then frozen and \TypedModelSeeds{} model seeds were trained, each with its own aggregated data. The test panel has \TypedTraceSeeds{} fresh trace seeds for each of \TypedScenarios{} families, \TypedOodScenarios{} of them held out from all fitting, and was evaluated once. Every method sees identical traces and contrasts subtract matched outcomes.

Two fixed-ratio references are kept apart. The \emph{train-selected} fixed ratio is the base ratio, \TypedBaseRatio{}, chosen on ID tune episodes before any model was fitted. It is the only fixed ratio that could have been chosen without the evaluation data, and it is the comparator for every claim below. The \emph{hindsight-best} fixed ratio is the one, among the six options and 0.85, with the highest utility on the evaluated panel itself (\TypedAuditIdHindsightFixedRatio{} on ID families, \TypedAuditOodHindsightFixedRatio{} on held-out families). It is an oracle over constants, selected on test data, and is reported as a bound beside the per-decision hindsight oracle.

Uncertainty is reported at two levels. \emph{Seed-level} intervals resample model seeds and trace seeds jointly and hold the families fixed; they say how well a difference is measured on these particular families. \emph{Family-level} intervals treat the families as the sample. A hierarchical bootstrap resamples families, traces within each drawn family, and model seeds; because a percentile bootstrap over four or seven clusters is anti-conservative, we also give a $t$ interval on the family-level mean differences and count the families whose difference is positive. A statement about unseen network families needs the family-level interval. Seven families give it little power, and that limit is part of the result.

\subsection{Broadened training and an untouched test set}
\label{sec:typed-broad}
The held-out result invites a broader training distribution, and the audit shows that seven families cannot settle a claim about unseen families. One further run addresses both. Its protocol was written down before any of its development or test results existed (\path{docs/JEVBWE_TYPED_V3_BROAD.md}).

\paragraph{Data roles.}
The seven formerly held-out families have now been read, and they motivated this run, so they are no longer called held-out. They become \emph{development} families: still never trained on, read before the test panel, and evaluated on the traces of the frozen panel above. Training uses \TypedBroadTrainFamilies{} families: the four legacy ID families and nine randomised ones that vary the capacity shape (several steps, piecewise ramps, drift, dips) and add the impairments the first run never trained on (loss episodes, deep queues, delay shifts, feedback gaps and dead links, large headroom). These knowingly cover the mechanisms of the development families. The test set is twelve new families, written together with the training families and before any controller ran on them. Each differs from every training family in temporal structure (sawtooth and square waves, a descending staircase, a smooth fade, many sub-second outages, rapid feedback flapping, a staged compound event) or in parameter range (capacity below 1\,Mbps, 120--180\,ms base delay, a buffer a quarter to half as deep, persistent 2--4.5\% random loss, jitter storms). They reuse the simulator's trace signals, so they are unseen families and not unseen physics. The configuration loader rejects a test family in any fitting or development role.

\paragraph{What changed.}
Only the training distribution changed: 1{,}300 episodes per aggregation round over thirteen families, with the features, labels, heads, optimiser, rules, guards and seeds of the frozen configuration. No new architecture was introduced. The fixed ratio re-selected on the training families is \TypedBroadBaseRatio{}, the top of the option set, so the head's only available improvement is a selective move below its base; the option set was not extended, and a base at the edge of the options is a limitation of this run. The first launch stopped during round-one data collection on a defect in the long-memory features, an empty window after a feedback gap. The fix cannot alter an episode that ran before, and the frozen panel above still replays exactly. On the training families' validation panel the head reaches \TypedBroadValBayesUtility{} against \TypedBroadValBaseUtility{} for the train-selected ratio, a gain of \TypedBroadValBayesVsBase{} \TypedBroadValBayesVsBaseInterval{} with ECE \TypedBroadValBayesEce{}.

\paragraph{Criteria fixed in advance.}
For the payoff-weighted proper-score RLCD head against the train-selected fixed ratio on the test panel: (1) \emph{gain}, claimed only if both family-level intervals exclude zero; (2) \emph{calibration}, called reasonable if pooled ECE is at most 0.05; (3) \emph{constraints}, called acceptable if the upper end of the family-level interval of the change in unsafe intervals is at most one percentage point. \subsection{What Study 10 shows}
Simulator counterfactual rollout supervision lets a small probabilistic bitrate policy outperform fixed-ratio control in distribution, in both runs and at family level. On families it has not trained on, its mean gain over the train-selected fixed ratio is positive in both runs, \TypedAuditOodBayesVsTrain{} on seven held-out families and \TypedBroadTestOodBayesVsTrain{} on twelve untouched families after broadened training, and in neither run does the family-level $t$ interval exclude zero. Generalisation and calibration degrade on unseen network families; broadened training narrows the calibration gap and lowers constraint violations without settling either question. RLCD-style training shows no advantage over exact-gradient or regression baselines on any panel, so there is currently no evidence that it causes the gain. What the evidence supports, in the simulator and in distribution, is counterfactual rollout supervision combined with payoff-sensitive, state-dependent control: the payoff-weighted heads and the advantage regression all act on predicted payoffs given the state, and rules that execute the most probable option keep much less. Every label, payoff and oracle in this study comes from simulator rollouts, which no controller receives at run time and which a real network cannot provide. The broadened run does not meet the target fixed in advance, and the typed controller is not promoted. No native-network learned gain has been demonstrated, and this study leaves the factual native-system identification problem where it was.

\section{Study 11: a stronger comparator, a sampled family population and factual supervision}
\label{sec:typed-followup}
Study 10 left three questions that need no new model. Was the fixed ratio a weak comparator? Is the small gain on unseen families real, and which families does a family-level interval speak for? And how much of what rollout supervision buys could a real network supply, given that it shows only the outcome of the option that ran? This study takes them in turn. It is synthetic. It reuses the frozen models and configuration of the broadened run of Section~\ref{sec:typed-broad} and fits no new typed head.

\subsection{Is the fixed ratio a weak comparator?}
\label{sec:typed-rules}
Two things about the comparator of Study 10 were unresolved. The train-selected ratio, \TypedBroadBaseRatio{}, is the top of the option set, so a higher ratio might have been better and the bar truncated by the grid. And a fixed ratio never reacts, so a few hand-written lines that back off after congestion might recover the head's gain.

\paragraph{Extended ratios.}
Ratios of 1.10 to 1.50 were added on the \TypedRuleTuneTraces{} training-family tune traces, with the governor's ceiling raised to match; for the six frozen options the raised ceiling changes no outcome. Utility is \TypedRuleTuneBase{} at \TypedBroadBaseRatio{}, \TypedRuleTuneAbove{} at 1.10 and \TypedRuleTuneTop{} at 1.50. The selected ratio is an interior optimum, and on the validation, development and former test panels it is also the best of the ten ratios in hindsight. The bar would not have been higher with a wider grid.

\paragraph{Tuned reactive rules.}
A class of rules was declared before any of them ran on a panel: back off for a hold time after congestion evidence; hysteresis on round-trip delay in excess of its running floor; a cap at a multiple of the rate delivered just before the last congestion onset; and a ladder that steps one option down on evidence and one up after a quiet hold. Each rule picks from the head's six options at the head's one-second cadence, passes through the same governor and sees only causal telemetry, and the congestion test and capacity knee are the ones the head's features use. The grid has \TypedRuleCandidates{} candidates. The comparator is the single candidate with the best mean utility on the tune traces: \TypedRuleBestName{}. Its tune utility, \TypedRuleTuneBest{}, is \TypedRuleTuneGain{} above the fixed ratio and is an optimistic maximum over the grid.

\subsection{Replication on a sampled family population}
\label{sec:typed-replication}
Twelve hand-written families are too few to separate a gain of 0.05 from zero when families differ by 0.09, and they are a convenience sample: an interval over them does not say which families it speaks for. A registered replication addresses both without a new model (\path{docs/JEVBWE_TYPED_V3_REPLICATION.md}).

\paragraph{Population.}
A family is one draw of family-level parameters, and an episode is one draw of a trace given them. The draws are independent: one of eight capacity shapes; mean capacity log-uniform between 0.34 and 3.6\,Mbps; base round-trip time log-uniform between 20 and 180\,ms; buffer depth log-uniform between a quarter and ten times the default; persistent 2--4.5\% random loss in 15\% of families; and a capacity surge, delay shift, jitter storm, loss episode, feedback gaps and outages each present independently in a quarter of families with a family-level severity. The ranges are those the hand-written families already use, so the population tests unseen families and not unseen physics. Three disjoint index spaces are fixed by a registered seed and a hash of the parameter table: \TypedRepFamilies{} families for this replication, \TypedRepSealedFamilies{} sealed for the next confirmatory experiment, and \TypedRepPoolFamilies{} that fitting may draw from. The configuration loader rejects either of the first two in a fitting or development role. The population was written after the test results of Study 10 had been read. The mechanisms of the three families that had proved insensitive to the controlled ratio were kept at stated rates, and before registration no controller, fixed ratio or oracle had run on any population family.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
The five frozen model files were identified by hash and evaluated once on \TypedRepFamilies{} families and \TypedRepTraceSeeds{} trace seeds (\TypedRepTraces{} traces); the command refuses to run if a model or the population differs from the registration. Four criteria were fixed in advance for the payoff-weighted RLCD head: (1) a gain over the train-selected fixed ratio with both family-level intervals excluding zero; (2) the same against the tuned rule of Section~\ref{sec:typed-rules}; (3) pooled ECE at most 0.05; and (4) an upper interval end of at most one percentage point for the change in unsafe intervals. Criteria 1, 3 and 4 and their thresholds are those of Study 10. A subset analysis was registered as diagnostic only: families where the hindsight oracle gains at least \TypedRepSensitiveMargin{} over the fixed ratio, a definition that involves no learned method.

\subsection{Factual supervision: what one option per decision can teach}
\label{sec:typed-factual}
Every learned result so far uses hindsight rollouts: at each decision the simulator is forked and all six options are played against the same future. A real network shows the outcome of the one option that ran. A native learned controller therefore depends on a question the simulator can answer and a native system cannot: how much of the rollout-supervised gain survives when the learner sees only factual outcomes, and how much randomised link time does that take? The design and its reading rules were written before the study ran (\path{docs/JEVBWE_TYPED_FACTUAL_V1.md}). It is a development study on the thirteen training families of Section~\ref{sec:typed-broad} and makes no confirmatory claim.

\paragraph{Design.}
Episodes run the base ratio on fresh training-family traces. At a free decision epoch a block starts with probability one half: one option is drawn uniformly, held for the commit window and followed by the base ratio for the tail, and the label is the mean utility of the block. This is the estimand of a rollout label observed for a single option, and on every row that has both, the two agree exactly. The \emph{factual pipeline} uses states, executed options and block outcomes only. A network with one value output per option is fitted by least squares on the executed option, with early stopping. On held-out blocks a doubly-robust estimate of policy value then selects how large a predicted advantage must be before the head leaves the base ratio; if no margin has a positive estimated gain, the head never acts. The \emph{rollout control} uses the same rows, split and network but is trained on the rollout advantage of all six options, so the two differ in the label source only. Budgets are nested, from \TypedFactOneBlocks{} to \TypedFactFiveBlocks{} blocks, which is \TypedFactOneHours{} to \TypedFactFiveHours{} hours of link time at \TypedFactBlocksPerEpisode{} blocks per episode, with \TypedFactSeeds{} data-and-optimiser seeds. The control is trained up to \TypedFactControlBlocks{} blocks, because a rollout label costs about forty times a factual block to compute. Both are deployed through the unchanged greedy rule and guards and scored on the validation traces, paired with the frozen fixed ratio.

\paragraph{Reading.}
Factual supervision is identifiable in this simulator, and expensive. The gain the control has at \TypedFactOneHours{} hours is first matched by the factual pipeline at \TypedFactFourHours{} hours, roughly sixty times the link time, because rollouts compare options on a shared future and factual blocks do not. Two cautions apply to the upper end of the curve. Early stopping ended every fit within a few passes over the data, and at the largest budget the offline advantage is still rising, so the flat stretch between the last two budgets may belong to the optimiser and not to the data. And the control, with a few thousand labelled states and no on-policy aggregation, is within 0.01 of the frozen regression head, so in distribution the rollout budget of Study 10 was far past saturation. For a real network the link time found here is a floor and not an estimate: the simulator is deterministic given the trace, telemetry is exact, utility is observed without error, the families are thirteen and known, the encoder follows the request, and nothing in this study tests unseen families.

\subsection{Per-link identification: a crossover that does not work}
\label{sec:typed-crossover}
On the replication panel a constant ratio chosen per family in hindsight gains \TypedRepFamilyConstantGain{} over the fixed ratio, \TypedRepFamilyConstantShare\% of the oracle's headroom, and the frozen head's gain differs from that by \TypedRepHeadVsFamilyConstant{}: much of what can be gained on an unseen link is knowing which constant suits it. A sender that meets the same link session after session could learn that from its own outcomes, with no offline training. The obstacle is that of Section~\ref{sec:typed-factual} in miniature. Between sessions on one link, utility at a fixed ratio has a standard deviation of \TypedCrossBetweenSpread{}, while the effect of moving from \TypedCrossPrimaryA{} to \TypedCrossPrimaryB{} has a standard deviation of \TypedCrossPrimarySpread{} across links. Comparing whole sessions run at one ratio with sessions run at the other needs \TypedCrossPrimaryWhole{} sessions per ratio before the per-link effect stands two standard errors from zero; on identical traces, which only a simulator has, \TypedCrossPrimaryIdeal{} suffice.

A crossover session is the classical remedy: alternate blocks of the two ratios within a session, in a random order, and compare within the session. A development study checked it against the yardstick a real network lacks, the difference between the two constant ratios on identical traces, on the \TypedCrossLinks{} families of the development pool with \TypedCrossSessions{} sessions each, four pairs of ratios, blocks of 3 to 18\,s and washouts of 0 to 2\,s (\path{docs/JEVBWE_TYPED_CROSSOVER_V1.md}). The reading was fixed in advance: a design is usable for a pair if, across links, the regression of the crossover estimate on the true effect has a slope between 0.8 and 1.2 and an intercept within 0.005 of zero.

\subsection{What Study 11 shows}
The comparator of Study 10 was not weak: the fixed ratio is an interior optimum, and a tuned reactive rule over the same options and telemetry does not reproduce the head's gain. On families drawn from a stated population the frozen head's gain over the train-selected fixed ratio is established at family level for the first time, and it is small, concentrated in a minority of families, partly a lower average ratio, and not established against the best fixed ratio in hindsight. Calibration does not carry over: on unseen families confidence is \TypedRepConfidence{} where accuracy is \TypedRepAccuracy{}, and the calibration criterion fails as it did in Study 10. For the third panel in a row, training with the scoring-rule policy gradient is indistinguishable from exact-gradient and regression controls. What rollout supervision buys can in principle be learned from factual outcomes with randomised, propensity-known blocks, at about sixty times the link time in the easiest possible setting. The cheaper route of learning one link's best ratio by crossing over within a session does not work here: short blocks measure a different quantity and long blocks cost more than whole sessions. None of this is a native result. It narrows what a native study would have to show: an actuator that follows the request, a factual data budget of hundreds of hours or a design that needs fewer, and a gain on networks the learner has not seen. The typed controller is not promoted.

\subsection{Conservative acceptance of factual policies: a development test}

\subsection{What Study 12 shows}
With training families drawn from the test population, a rollout-supervised typed head meets every criterion fixed in advance on families no controller had run on: an established gain over a fixed ratio and over a tuned rule, pooled calibration within the threshold, and fewer constraint violations. Without rollouts, a factual learner trained on the same families gains on one of two unseen-family panels after hundreds of hours of randomised link time and loses utility at the smallest budget, with a selection step that failed to refuse it. The typed controller is not promoted.

\section{Limitations and next validation steps}
\paragraph{Simulation fidelity.}
The simulator models a single bottleneck with aggregate fluid service. It omits packet ordering, retransmission, codec dependencies, FEC block structure, competing adaptive flows and perceptual quality. It therefore does not assess multi-flow fairness or establish RFC compliance. Large virtual outage delays are not latency measurements from delivered traffic. The next external-validity steps are recorded network/content panels, a matched native RLCD integration and codec-aware QoE validation. Appendix~\ref{app:native-frames} adds an ancillary native WebRTC packet/codec and application-frame timing diagnostic, but it runs native browser control only, not RLCD; it does not externally validate these nine policy studies.

\paragraph{Distribution and statistical scope.}
Synthetic families with fixed event shapes are not representative Internet sampling. V1 partly reused pilot trace seeds. V2–v9 are adaptive follow-ups to known model behavior and do not restore an untouched research process. V2 and v4 expose candidate components to previously labeled stress mechanisms; v3–v9 final tests use disjoint realizations of the same known families. V5 screens alternatives with a calibrator fitted for original proposals. V6 refits on source-screen-selected ID actions, but the recalibrator can itself change later choices and states; OOD selected-action confidence remains optimistic. V7's fixed disagreement threshold was selected after prior OOD diagnostics and triggers rarely; its small test-panel risk shift is conditional on this known synthetic panel. V8 substantially changes action selection and occupancy, loses QoE on erasure-heavy conditions, and fails its nominal-quality promotion bound. Its confidence ablation retains learned Q values and support rejection, so it does not isolate all learned components. V9 removes raw-loss-only budget and fallback backoff on a new panel; burst-loss quality recovers but nominal quality still fails its promotion bound. Confidence eligibility has a small conditional stress regression versus the matched ablation, and selected-action stress scores remain optimistic. Delay evidence does not identify loss causes reliably or certify feasibility. Neither the telemetry-support heuristic, causal budget nor one-step risk score composes into episode safety. Conditional model-seed intervals omit new-network-population uncertainty. Future work needs independently collected traces and mechanisms, not repeated tuning against these panels.

\paragraph{Typed-decision study.}
Study~10 inherits the simulator limits above and adds its own. Its labels replay the true future trace in a forked simulator; on a real network only the executed option has an outcome, so a native version needs factual, propensity-logged data, and an earlier factual-only attempt at a few hundred cohorts was too small to say whether that works. ID families place their events at fixed fractions of an episode, which a learned controller can exploit; the summaries ablation suggests the long-memory features are not the channel, but the ordered history could still be. The estimator bound, option set and rollout horizon were chosen from sweeps that included the held-out families, although before any model was trained. The comparison is against fixed ratios over a simplified estimator, not against an adaptive production controller, and under this QoE function the legacy estimator's constant cap outscores every method over the bounded estimator while violating the constraint more often. The payoff table assumes an option's payoff depends on the history only through which option is best. Held-out confidence is over-optimistic, and neither the support guard nor a confidence threshold turns it into a guarantee. Pooled calibration hides family-level miscalibration even in distribution. Seven held-out and twelve test families are small samples of families, so family-level intervals are wide; three of the twelve test families turned out to be insensitive to the controlled ratio. The test families were written by the authors of the training families and reuse the simulator's trace signals, so they probe unseen families and not unseen physics. In the broadened run the train-selected ratio sits at the top of the option set, and the two acceptance thresholds for calibration and constraints are judgment calls.

\paragraph{Safety semantics and attribution.}
One-step calibrated success does not compose automatically into episode safety. The fallback may itself introduce harm, and some network conditions are infeasible at every action. Evaluation-only counterfactuals attribute immediate effects from the same state but do not estimate the long-run causal benefit of a different controller trajectory. Formal shielding would require additional sound abstractions or invariant-set reasoning that this work does not provide.

\paragraph{Study 11.}
The rule audit of Section~\ref{sec:typed-rules} was designed after the development and former test panels of Study 10 had been read; only its last row, on the registered panel, is confirmatory. The rule class is small and acts at the head's cadence over the head's options. The family population of Section~\ref{sec:typed-replication} is a declared distribution over the simulator's existing signals, written by an author who had seen the Study 10 results; it defines which families an interval speaks for and adds no physics, and its realism is not established. The replication evaluates models trained on hand-written families, so its small gain mixes a transfer gap with any limit of the method; Study 12 separates the two. The factual study is in distribution, uses one reasonable pipeline and one optimiser setting, collects data under the base policy only, and observes a noiseless utility; its link-time figures are specific to this simulator and reward. The crossover study uses 36\,s sessions that each start cold, on a population whose time scales are defined relative to the session, so it says nothing about sessions of several minutes with a warm estimator.

\paragraph{Study 12.}
The sealed panel was evaluated once and its four criteria were fixed beforehand, but the population, the panel size and the thresholds are the authors' choices, and the calibration criterion is a pooled one that the family-level figures show to be lenient. The development and sealed panels are two samples of one population and give gains of \TypedPopDevGain{} and \TypedPopGain{}; the sealed figure is the registered result and overstates the effect if read alone. The comparison with the heads of Study 10 holds the method fixed and varies the training families, the base-ratio tuning traces and the calibration episodes together, so it does not say which of them moved calibration. No sealed panel remains; a further confirmatory experiment needs a newly registered index space of the population. The factual study on unseen families scores on the two panels that had already been read and was designed after their results were known, so it is development evidence; its two panels disagree, and its doubly-robust selection is one procedure among many.

\section{Conclusion}
RLCD provides a causal, auditable interface between learned preferences, empirical one-step safety probabilities and deterministic intervention. Gating improves the initial ungated policy's stress outcomes, but safety refits, randomized training and additional guards do not uniformly improve the complete controller. Action screening and ID selected-action recalibration improve nominal outcomes while leaving stress overconfidence; V7's disagreement guard has only a small conditional effect. V8 produces a much larger stress-violation reduction, but loses both nominal and stress QoE and is rejected by its frozen validation constraint. Its confidence ablation finds no resolved additional violation protection beyond the envelope, rather than establishing a general benefit from calibrated eligibility. V9 removes raw-loss-only budget and fallback backoff, recovering burst-loss quality relative to V8 with unchanged observed violations, but still fails nominal-quality promotion. Its confidence ablation reveals a small conditional stress regression from adding eligibility. The preserved decision is to retain V7, not choose a post-test winner. A separate bitrate-only study gives a narrower result for a different model class. A small typed head supervised by counterfactual simulator rollouts on its own trajectories outperforms fixed-ratio control in distribution, provided its probabilities are multiplied by a measured payoff table rather than thresholded. On families it did not train on, the mean gain over the train-selected ratio is positive but is not established once families are the unit of analysis, before and after the training distribution is broadened, and calibration degrades. An exact-gradient control and a regression baseline match the scoring-rule policy gradient, so the gain is not specific to that recipe. The labels require a simulator, and no native-network gain has been demonstrated. A follow-up without a new model sharpens this. A tuned reactive rule does not reproduce the head's gain, so the comparator was not weak. On \TypedRepFamilies{} families drawn from a stated population the frozen head's gain over the train-selected ratio is established for the first time, at \TypedRepGain{}, about a third of the earlier estimate and not established against the best fixed ratio in hindsight, and its probabilities are over-confident. Learning from factual outcomes alone is possible in the simulator and costs roughly sixty times the link time of rollout labels, and a within-session crossover does not identify one link's best ratio more cheaply. With training families drawn from the population, heads trained by the same method meet all four criteria fixed in advance on a sealed panel, at a gain of \TypedPopGain{} that is a quarter of the hindsight oracle's; the change of training families improves pooled calibration and constraint violations and not utility, family-level calibration remains poor, and an untyped regression of advantages outperforms the typed head.\NOneConclusionSentence{} Independent network populations, congestion-versus-erasure validation, episode-level risk calibration, and packet/codec experiments are needed before deployment claims. These synthetic adaptive findings provide neither a formal safety guarantee nor unseen-mechanism or real-network validation.
